How to make your own coffee

Last updated: November 23, 2026

The daily café coffee is rarely about the coffee. It’s the walk, the routine, the small reward, and the fact that it’s right on the way.

Why it’s worth it

A coffee made at home costs a fraction of one bought at a counter, and the difference adds up quickly when it’s every day. Once you have a cup you like at home, the café becomes a treat again instead of a habit you pay for.

How to start

  1. Make coffee you actually like. If the café cup is better than yours, fix that first: fresher beans, a grinder, a French press or a moka pot. You don’t need an expensive machine.
  2. Get a good travel mug. One that keeps coffee hot and doesn’t leak in your bag. It makes the homemade cup just as portable.
  3. Prepare it the night before. Set up the coffee maker, or leave the beans and the mug out on the counter, so the morning takes one step.
  4. Keep the ritual, change the place. If what you like is the break, take it anyway: go for a short walk with your own cup at the same time of day.
  5. Plan your café visits. Pick one day a week to go, with a friend or on Friday. A planned coffee is a treat, not a slip.

When it gets hard

  • The café is on your way. Have your own cup already in hand when you walk past. It’s hard to buy a coffee while holding one.
  • Coworkers invite you. Go along for the walk and the talk, and bring your mug or order something small.
  • You buy one anyway. Log the slip and keep going. One coffee doesn’t erase the money you’ve already kept.
