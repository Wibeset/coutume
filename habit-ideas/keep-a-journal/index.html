How to keep a journal

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Most journals die in the second week, after a few long, careful entries. The problem isn’t a lack of things to say: it’s that every page starts to feel like homework.

Why it’s worth it

Three lines a day is small enough to do on any night, and over a few months it adds up to a record of your life you’d never have kept otherwise. Writing down the day also helps you set it down before you sleep.

How to start

  1. Write three lines, no more. A short limit makes it easy to start and easy to finish. If you want to write more some nights, you can.
  2. Pick your place. A small notebook on the nightstand, or the Notes app on your phone. Paper has the advantage of keeping screens out of bed.
  3. Use the same three prompts. What happened today, one thing that went well, one thing on your mind. You never face a blank page.
  4. Write at the same moment. Right after getting into bed, or with your evening tea. The moment does the remembering.
  5. Don’t reread for a while. Write for yourself in a month or a year, not for today. Rereading too soon makes you want to polish it.

When it gets hard

  • Nothing happened today. Write that. “Quiet day, soup for supper, tired” is a perfectly good entry.
  • You fall behind. Don’t catch up on missed days. Just write tonight’s three lines and carry on.
  • It feels private. Keep the notebook somewhere only you look, or lock your notes. You’ll write more honestly.
