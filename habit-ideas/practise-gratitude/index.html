How to practise gratitude every day

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Most gratitude habits fade because they turn into a list of the same three things: family, health, coffee. The habit lasts when you look for something specific to today.

Why it’s worth it

Writing down one good thing a day trains you to notice them while they happen, not just to remember the hard parts. It takes a minute, and on rough days the page reminds you that the day wasn’t only rough.

How to start

  1. Keep it to one thing. One line is enough. A short habit you do every day beats a long one you drop after a week.
  2. Be specific. Not “my friends” but “Julie called just to check how my appointment went.” Details make you relive the moment.
  3. Write it in the same place. A small notebook by your bed or a single note in the Notes app on your phone. Having everything in one spot lets you reread it later.
  4. Pick a fixed moment. Before bed works for many people, since you can look back over the whole day. Right after dinner works too.
  5. Reread now and then. Flip back through a month of entries on a slow Sunday. It’s often the most rewarding part.

When it gets hard

  • You can’t think of anything. Go small: a hot shower, a good song, a bus that came on time. Small counts.
  • It feels fake on a bad day. You don’t have to feel grateful to write. Just name one thing that went okay.
  • You miss a day. Don’t write two tomorrow. Write your one line and carry on.
