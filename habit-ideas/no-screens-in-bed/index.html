How to stop using your phone in bed

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Nobody plans to scroll for an hour in bed. The phone is just there, within reach, and it never runs out of things to show you.

Why it’s worth it

When the bed is only for sleep and reading, getting into it starts to feel like the end of the day. You fall asleep with your own thoughts instead of someone else’s feed, and you get back the time the scrolling used to take.

How to start

  1. Charge your phone outside the bedroom. Or at least across the room. If you have to get up to reach it, you mostly won’t.
  2. Get a real alarm clock. “I need it for my alarm” is the main reason phones end up in bed. A simple clock removes it.
  3. Put it away at a set time. Pick a moment, like brushing your teeth, after which the phone stays on its charger until morning.
  4. Give your hands something else. A paper book, a magazine or a crossword on the nightstand fills the gap the phone leaves.
  5. Do your last checks before bed. Look at tomorrow’s weather, answer the message, set the alarm in the kitchen. Nothing is left to check once you lie down.

When it gets hard

  • You reach for it out of reflex. That’s why distance works better than willpower. Make the reach impossible, not forbidden.
  • You can’t fall asleep without something on. Try an audiobook or quiet music started from another room, or a book with a small reading light.
  • You slip. Log it and move on. One night with the phone doesn’t undo the nights you went without.
