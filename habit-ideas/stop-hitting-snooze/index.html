How to stop hitting snooze

Last updated: November 21, 2026

The snooze button makes a promise it can’t keep: nine more minutes of rest. What you mostly get is broken sleep and a morning that starts with a small argument with yourself.

Why it’s worth it

Getting up on the first alarm gives you a calmer start and the time you were trying to buy with snooze. It also replaces a daily negotiation with a simple rule: when it rings, you’re up.

How to start

  1. Set the alarm for the real time. If you always snooze until 7:10, set it for 7:10. Use the time you actually need, not a time you hope for.
  2. Put it across the room. If you have to stand up to turn it off, you’ve already done the hard part.
  3. Plan the first two minutes. Feet on the floor, open the curtains, drink a glass of water. A small, fixed sequence beats deciding while half-asleep.
  4. Turn snooze off. Most alarms let you disable it. If the button isn’t there, there’s nothing to press.
  5. Work on the night before. Snoozing often means you went to bed too late. Moving bedtime earlier, even by fifteen minutes, makes the morning easier.

When it gets hard

  • It’s dark and cold. Keep a sweater by the bed and turn on a light as soon as you’re up.
  • You turn it off and go back to bed. Leave the bedroom right away: kitchen, bathroom, anywhere but the bed.
  • You slipped. Get up now, log the slip, and don’t let one late start ruin the day. Look at last night’s bedtime, then try again tomorrow.
