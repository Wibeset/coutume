How to cancel subscriptions you don’t use

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Subscriptions are designed to be forgotten. Each one is small, renews on its own, and is easier to keep than to think about.

Why it’s worth it

Cancelling one unused subscription saves money every month without changing anything in your day. And once you’ve made the list, you know exactly what you pay for, which makes the next decision easier.

How to start

  1. List every recurring charge. Go through two or three months of bank and credit card statements and note anything that comes back: streaming, apps, cloud storage, boxes, memberships.
  2. Check your iPhone. Go to Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions to see everything billed through your Apple Account, with renewal dates.
  3. Ask one question per line. Did I use this in the last month? If not, cancel it. You can always sign up again if you miss it.
  4. Cancel before the renewal date. Do it today rather than “before the end of the month”. Most services let you keep access until the period you paid for ends.
  5. Watch for free trials. When you start one, decide right away if you’ll keep it, and cancel the same day if not. You usually keep the trial anyway.

When it gets hard

  • “I might use it.” Cancel it and see if you notice. Missing something for a month is the best test there is.
  • Cancelling is hidden. Look in the account or billing settings on the website, not the app. If you’re stuck, contact the service and ask them to cancel.
  • New ones keep appearing. That’s where the habit comes in: treat every new subscription as a decision, not a click.

Track it in Coutume

Cancelling is a one-off, so in Coutume add “No new subscription” as a habit to quit. It’s kept by default and you only log the days you sign up for something new; Coutume counts the days without and shows the money saved.

Check each service’s cancellation terms, since some annual plans aren’t refunded.
