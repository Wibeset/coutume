How to take the stairs every day

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Nobody plans to take the elevator. You walk into the lobby, the doors are open, and the decision is made before you notice. Taking the stairs is about deciding before you get there.

Why it’s worth it

Stairs are exercise already built into your day: no time to find, no clothes to change. A few flights get your heart and legs working, and after a few weeks they become something you no longer think about.

How to start

  1. Decide once. Make a rule like “under four floors, I walk” so you don’t debate it every time.
  2. Start with part of the way. If you work on the twelfth floor, take the elevator to the eighth and walk the rest. Get off lower as it gets easier.
  3. Know where the stairs are. At work, in the subway, at the mall. Stairs you can find quickly are stairs you’ll take.
  4. Go at your own pace. Hold the railing, stop on a landing if you need to. Nobody is timing you.
  5. Count each climb. Logging every time you take the stairs turns small choices into something you can see add up.

When it gets hard

  • You’re carrying bags. Take the elevator with the groceries, then walk down or up once without them.
  • You arrive out of breath. Leave a minute earlier and climb more slowly. It gets easier with time.
  • There are no stairs in your day. Find some: the ones at home, in a park, in a building near work. Two or three flights up and down count.
