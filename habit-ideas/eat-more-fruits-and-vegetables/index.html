How to eat more fruits and vegetables

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Fruits and vegetables rarely lose to snacks on taste: they lose on convenience. A bag of chips is ready to eat; a head of broccoli needs washing, cutting and a plan.

Why it’s worth it

Adding one fruit or vegetable to each meal is a small change that needs no diet and doesn’t take anything away. Your plate gets more colourful, and it’s an easy rule to check: one look at your plate tells you.

How to start

  1. Add, don’t replace. Keep eating what you like and put something next to it: berries on your cereal, a handful of spinach in your pasta, an apple with your sandwich.
  2. Wash and cut ahead. Take 15 minutes after groceries to wash the grapes and cut carrots and peppers. Food that’s ready to eat gets eaten.
  3. Put them where you look. A fruit bowl on the counter, cut vegetables at the front of the fridge, at eye level.
  4. Use frozen and canned. Frozen fruit and vegetables keep for months and go straight into soups, stir-fries and smoothies. They count too.
  5. Start with breakfast. It’s the easiest meal to change: a banana, a few berries, or tomato on toast.

When it gets hard

  • Produce goes bad before you eat it. Buy less, more often, and lean on frozen for the rest of the week.
  • You eat out or on the go. Pick the side salad or the vegetables, or keep a piece of fruit in your bag.
  • You don’t like vegetables. Start with the ones you tolerate, try them roasted, or hide them in sauces and soups. Fruit counts too.
