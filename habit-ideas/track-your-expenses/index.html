How to track your expenses

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Most people know roughly what their rent costs and have no idea where the rest goes. The big bills are easy to remember; it’s the small ones that disappear.

Why it’s worth it

Writing down what you spend is the simplest way to see where your money actually goes, before deciding anything about it. You don’t need a budget to start: just the list, and a few weeks of honesty.

How to start

  1. Pick one place. A note on your phone, a small notebook or a spreadsheet. The best tool is the one you’ll open every day.
  2. Write it down right after paying. Amount, place, one word for what it was. It takes ten seconds at the counter and nothing to remember later.
  3. Count everything. The $3 coffee, the parking meter, the app purchase. Small expenses are the whole point of the exercise.
  4. Review at the end of the day. Compare your list with your banking app to catch what you missed, especially card payments.
  5. Look for patterns after a few weeks. Group your expenses into a few rough categories. Don’t judge them yet: just notice what surprises you.

When it gets hard

  • You forget to write things down. Rely on the evening review. Your bank statement remembers what you didn’t.
  • Cash slips through. Keep receipts in one pocket of your wallet and empty it when you do your review.
  • Seeing the total is uncomfortable. That’s normal at first. The list is information, not a verdict, and it’s what lets you change things.
