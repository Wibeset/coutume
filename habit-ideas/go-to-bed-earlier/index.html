How to go to bed earlier

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Going to bed late is rarely a decision. It’s the episode that ends at 11:40, the dishes, one more message, and suddenly it’s past midnight.

Why it’s worth it

An earlier bedtime gives you more room for sleep without touching your alarm. Mornings get calmer, and the evening stops being a race against the clock that you always lose.

How to start

  1. Work back from your alarm. If you get up at 7, being in bed by 11 leaves time to fall asleep and still get a full night. Pick the time that fits your mornings, not someone else’s.
  2. Move it 15 minutes at a time. If you usually get to bed at midnight, aim for 11:45 this week and 11:30 the next. Small shifts are easier to keep than one big jump.
  3. Start winding down earlier. Bedtime begins about half an hour before you lie down: dishes done, lights lower, teeth brushed. Pick the moment that kicks it off, like 10:30 or the end of the supper cleanup.
  4. Decide before you press play. Choose how many episodes you’ll watch before you start, and stop at the end of one, not in the middle of the next.
  5. Keep it close on weekends. Staying up two hours later on Friday and Saturday makes Sunday night harder. Staying within an hour of your usual time is a good rule.

When it gets hard

  • You get a second wind at 10 p.m. That’s the moment to start winding down, not to start something new. Dim the lights and leave the chores for tomorrow.
  • Evenings are your only free time. Move the thing you love earlier, like reading or a show right after supper, so you don’t take it out of your sleep.
  • You can’t fall asleep right away. Lying in bed calmly still counts. Keep the same time for a few weeks; your body tends to follow.
