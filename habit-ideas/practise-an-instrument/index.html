How to practise an instrument every day

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Most instruments that go unplayed aren’t abandoned on purpose. They just live in a case in the closet, and getting them out becomes one step too many.

Why it’s worth it

Playing a little every day builds the finger memory that a long session once a week can’t. It also gives you something that’s yours alone, a stretch of time where you’re making something instead of scrolling.

How to start

  1. Leave the instrument out. A guitar on a stand, the piano lid open, the ukulele on the couch. If you can pick it up in five seconds, you will.
  2. Set a small minimum. Ten minutes, or even one song. Most days you’ll keep going once you start.
  3. Warm up, then work on one thing. A scale or a song you know, then the one passage that trips you up. Play it slowly until it’s clean, then speed up.
  4. End with something you enjoy. Finish on a piece you like playing, so you look forward to tomorrow’s session.
  5. Record yourself now and then. Listening back a month later shows you progress you don’t hear while playing.

When it gets hard

  • Practice feels like homework. Learn a song you actually want to play, even if it’s simple. Fun keeps you coming back.
  • You worry about the neighbours. Play at reasonable hours, use a practice mute, or plug headphones into a digital instrument.
  • You miss a day. Your hands haven’t forgotten anything. Pick it up tomorrow and play.
