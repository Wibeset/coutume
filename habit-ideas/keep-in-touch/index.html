How to keep in touch with people you care about

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Most friendships don’t end, they drift. Nobody decides to stop calling; the gaps just get longer until reaching out starts to feel awkward.

Why it’s worth it

A short message a day keeps your people close without needing a free evening. Small, regular contact also makes the bigger moments easier: it’s simpler to ask for help, or offer it, when you talked last week.

How to start

  1. Make a short list. Write down the people you’d like to hear from more often: family, old friends, a former colleague. Ten names is plenty.
  2. Reach one person a day. Go down the list. A text, a voice message, a photo that reminded you of them. It doesn’t have to be a long call.
  3. Pick a moment. On your walk home, while the kettle boils, during lunch. A regular moment keeps it from slipping.
  4. Be specific. “Saw a dog just like Max today” opens a conversation more easily than “How are you?”
  5. Don’t keep score. Some people will answer days later, some won’t. You’re reaching out because you care, not to get a reply.

When it gets hard

  • It’s been too long and it feels awkward. Say so lightly: “I’ve been meaning to write for ages.” Most people are just glad to hear from you.
  • You don’t know what to say. Share something small: a song, a memory, a question about their week.
  • You miss a day. No one is counting. Send tomorrow’s message and carry on.
