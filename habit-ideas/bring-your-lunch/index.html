How to bring your lunch

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Almost everyone means to bring a lunch. What gets in the way isn’t cooking: it’s standing in front of the fridge at 7:30 a.m. with nothing ready and no time to think.

Why it’s worth it

A lunch from home usually costs a fraction of one bought near work, and you know what’s in it. It also frees your lunch break from line-ups, so you get the whole break to eat, walk or rest.

How to start

  1. Cook once, eat twice. Make a little more at supper and put a portion straight into a container before you serve. Tomorrow’s lunch is done before the dishes.
  2. Keep a few defaults. Two or three lunches you can make in five minutes, like a wrap, a big salad or soup from the freezer, for the days with no leftovers.
  3. Pack it the night before. Put the container in the fridge and your bag by the door. In the morning, you only need to grab it.
  4. Get containers you like. Ones that don’t leak, fit in your bag and go in the microwave. A bad container is a surprisingly good excuse.
  5. Start with three days a week. Pick the days you know you’re home the evening before. Add more once those feel easy.

When it gets hard

  • There are no leftovers. Keep a frozen backup: soups, chili or burritos in single portions you can grab on the way out.
  • Your coworkers go out to eat. Join them once a week and bring your lunch the other days. You don’t have to choose all or nothing.
  • You get bored. Change one thing at a time: a new sauce, a different bread, fruit you don’t usually buy.
