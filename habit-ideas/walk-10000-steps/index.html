How to walk 10,000 steps a day

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Ten thousand steps sounds like a long walk, and a busy day has no room for one. But most of your steps don’t come from one big walk: they come from a dozen small ones.

Why it’s worth it

Walking needs no gym, no gear and no skill, and it fits into days that have no space for a workout. A step count is also easy to measure, so you always know where you stand. And a short walk is one of the simplest ways to clear your head in the middle of the day.

How to start

  1. Start from where you are. Look at your average for the past week. If it’s around 4,000, aim for 6,000 first and raise the target once it feels easy.
  2. Attach a walk to a meal. Ten minutes after lunch or after dinner, around the block or to the end of the street and back. It becomes part of the meal instead of one more thing to fit in.
  3. Turn errands into steps. Park at the far end of the lot, get off the bus one stop early, walk to the corner store instead of driving.
  4. Walk while you talk. Take phone calls on your feet, pace while you listen to a podcast, suggest a walk instead of a coffee with a friend.
  5. Check your count before dinner. If you’re short, a walk in the early evening usually closes the gap while there’s still time.

When it gets hard

  • The weather is bad. Walk in a mall, climb the stairs of your building, or pace at home while you listen to something.
  • You work at a screen all day. Get up once an hour and take the long way: the farthest washroom, the other floor, the water fountain at the end of the hall.
  • You fall short. Seven thousand steps is still a good day. If you miss your target most days, lower it for now and raise it later.
