How to remember to take your vitamins

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Forgetting your vitamins is rarely about not caring. It’s the opposite: it’s so routine that by noon you can’t remember whether you took them this morning or yesterday.

Why it’s worth it

Vitamins and daily medication are meant to be taken regularly, and the hard part is rarely swallowing them: it’s remembering. A fixed time and a quick log take the guesswork out, so you never have to wonder whether you already took them.

How to start

  1. Pick one time and keep it. Breakfast, lunch or bedtime: whichever moment you never skip. The same time every day.
  2. Tie it to something you already do. Next to the coffee maker, by your toothbrush, beside your plate. The habit you already have reminds you of the new one.
  3. Keep the bottle in sight. Take it out of the cupboard and put it where you’ll see it at that moment, but out of reach of children and pets.
  4. Use a weekly pill organizer. One compartment per day shows you at a glance whether today’s dose is taken.
  5. Log it right away. Mark it the moment you take it, not later. That’s how you’ll know for sure at bedtime.

When it gets hard

  • You can’t remember whether you took it. Check your organizer or your log. Don’t take a second dose “just in case”; for medication, ask your pharmacist what to do.
  • Your routine changes on weekends or trips. Tie it to something that doesn’t change, like brushing your teeth, and pack the organizer in your toiletry bag.
  • You run out. Check what’s left when you fill the organizer, and buy more before the last week.
