How to quit sugary drinks

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Soda and juice rarely feel like a habit. They feel like a treat, a pick-me-up, or just what comes with the meal, which is why they’re so easy to drink without deciding to.

Why it’s worth it

“No soda, no juice” is a clear rule you can keep without counting anything. Many people find they spend less at the corner store, and that water and unsweetened drinks start to feel like enough.

How to start

  1. Name your moments. Notice when you usually grab one: with lunch, at 3 p.m., with takeout, at the movies. Most people have two or three, not twenty.
  2. Pick a replacement for each. Sparkling water, unsweetened iced tea, water with lemon or cucumber. Something cold and fizzy often does the job the soda did.
  3. Stop stocking it. A case in the fridge gets drunk. Finish it or give it away, and keep the replacement cold instead.
  4. Order before you look. At a counter or a drive-through, ask for water before the menu offers you a combo with a drink.
  5. Decide about the in-between drinks. Sweetened coffees, iced teas and sports drinks don’t feel like soda, but they can be part of the same habit. Choose ahead whether they count for you.

When it gets hard

  • The afternoon craving. It often fades within a few minutes. Drink a glass of cold sparkling water, walk around the block, then see if you still want it.
  • Everyone else is having one. Hold a glass of something else. Nobody checks what’s in it.
  • You slipped. Log it, skip the second can “since the day is ruined anyway,” and start again at the next meal. One soda doesn’t erase the days before it.
