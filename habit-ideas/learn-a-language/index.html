How to practise a language 15 minutes a day

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Most people start a language with a burst of enthusiasm and a two-hour session, then don’t open the book again for a month. A language rewards the opposite: a little, often.

Why it’s worth it

Fifteen minutes a day keeps the words fresh, so you spend your time learning instead of relearning what you forgot. It also fits on the busiest days, which is what turns an intention into a habit.

How to start

  1. Tie it to something you do daily. With your morning coffee, on the train, while dinner cooks. A fixed moment removes the daily decision.
  2. Learn the words you need. Ordering food, talking about your work, asking directions. Words you’ll use stick better than lists from a textbook.
  3. Speak out loud from the start. Read sentences aloud, repeat after a recording, talk to yourself in the kitchen. Your mouth needs practice as much as your memory.
  4. Mix your sources. An app one day, a podcast the next, a song or a short show with subtitles. Variety keeps fifteen minutes from feeling like homework.
  5. Keep it to fifteen. On good days you can do more, but don’t make that the rule. A small daily dose is what lasts.

When it gets hard

  • You feel stuck. Progress in a language comes in plateaus. Look back at what you couldn’t say three months ago.
  • You’re shy about speaking. Start alone, then with a patient friend or a conversation exchange. Mistakes are how you learn.
  • You miss a day. Don’t double up tomorrow. Do your fifteen minutes and carry on.
