How to wait before buying

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Impulse purchases rarely feel like impulses. In the moment, the jacket on sale or the gadget in the ad feels like something you’ve wanted for a while.

Why it’s worth it

A day’s wait lets the excitement settle so you can decide with a clear head. Often you still want the thing, and you buy it without regret; sometimes you don’t, and you’ve kept the money without feeling deprived.

How to start

  1. Set your threshold. $50 is a good starting point: high enough to leave groceries and small things alone, low enough to catch most impulse buys.
  2. Leave it in the cart. Online, add it to the cart or a wish list and close the tab. In a store, take a photo of the item and the price, and walk out.
  3. Write down why you want it. One sentence: what it’s for and where it will go. Reading it again tomorrow tells you a lot.
  4. Look again after 24 hours. If you still want it and it fits your budget, buy it with a clear conscience. That’s a planned purchase, not a slip.
  5. Remove the shortcuts. Delete saved cards from shopping sites and unsubscribe from promotional emails. Every extra step gives you time to think.

When it gets hard

  • “It’s only on sale today.” Most sales come back. If this one doesn’t, it’s rarely the only chance to get something similar.
  • You buy to feel better. Notice when it happens: after a hard day, late at night. Do something else for ten minutes, and the urge often passes.
  • You give in. Log the slip and keep going. If you can still return it, the 24 hours can start now.
