How to read 20 pages a day

Last updated: November 23, 2026

Most people who stopped reading didn’t lose their taste for books: they lost the time slot. The evenings filled up with screens, and the book on the night table stayed on page 40.

Why it’s worth it

Twenty pages fit into almost any day, and over a year they add up to a good stack of books. Reading also gives you a quiet stretch away from screens, and many people only notice how much they missed it once it’s back.

How to start

  1. Pick a moment that already exists. On the bus, with your morning coffee, in bed before lights out. A fixed moment does more than a vague plan to read “when there’s time.”
  2. Keep the book where you’ll be. On your pillow, in your bag, next to the couch. If it takes effort to find, you’ll reach for your phone instead.
  3. Put the phone somewhere else. Leave it charging in another room while you read. Twenty pages go quickly when nothing buzzes.
  4. Read what you actually enjoy. Thrillers, comics, history, cookbooks: the habit matters more than what’s on the shelf.
  5. Keep count of your pages. Twenty is a floor, not a ceiling. A reading tracker such as Reliure, from the makers of Coutume, can keep track of your books and the pages you read in each.

When it gets hard

  • The book bores you. Put it down and start another. Finishing a book out of duty is the quickest way to stop reading altogether.
  • You fall asleep after two pages. Move your reading earlier: lunch break, commute, or right after dinner.
  • You miss a day. Don’t try to read forty pages tomorrow to make up for it. Just read your twenty.
