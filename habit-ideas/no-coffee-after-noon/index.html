How to stop drinking coffee after noon

Last updated: November 21, 2026

The afternoon coffee rarely feels like a choice. It’s the meeting, the slump after lunch, the break with a coworker, and by the time you lie awake at night, it’s long forgotten.

Why it’s worth it

Caffeine can stay in your body for several hours, so a 3 p.m. cup may still be with you at bedtime. A noon cutoff lets you keep your morning coffee and still give your evening a fair chance.

How to start

  1. Set one clear line. Noon is easy to remember and leaves plenty of morning. “Less coffee in the afternoon” is too vague to keep.
  2. Enjoy the morning cups. You’re not quitting coffee. Make the morning ones good, so the afternoon one feels less necessary.
  3. Keep the ritual, change the cup. If you like the break, keep the break. Make it herbal tea, sparkling water, or a decaf if it’s truly caffeine-free enough for you.
  4. Plan for the slump. The post-lunch dip often passes. A short walk outside, a glass of cold water or a few minutes of daylight can carry you through it.
  5. Watch the other sources. Tea, cola and energy drinks carry caffeine too. Decide whether your rule covers them.

When it gets hard

  • The afternoon meeting with coffee. Take a cup of tea or water. Holding something is usually what matters.
  • You slept badly and need it. Have an earlier, bigger morning coffee instead of a late one. A late cup tends to make the next night harder too.
  • You slipped. Log it and move on. Tomorrow at noon the line is there again, and one cup doesn’t cancel the days you held it.
