How to stop doomscrolling in bed

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Doomscrolling doesn’t feel like a choice while you do it. Each post is short, the feed never ends, and “one more minute” quietly becomes midnight.

Why it’s worth it

The last half hour before sleep is when you’re most tired and least able to stop. Taking the feed out of bed gives you back that time, and often a calmer head when the lights go out.

How to start

  1. Let the phone sleep somewhere else. Charge it in the kitchen or across the room. If you need an alarm, a simple clock does the job.
  2. Set a cutoff you can see. “Phone down when I brush my teeth” is easier to keep than a time you’ll argue with.
  3. Replace the last ten minutes. A paper book, a magazine, a podcast with the screen off, a few stretches. Your hands want something to do.
  4. Make the apps harder to open. Log out of the worst one at night, or move it off your Home Screen. A small pause is often enough.
  5. Get the news earlier. If you want to stay informed, read it once at lunch. Nothing in the news needs you at 11 p.m.

When it gets hard

  • You can’t fall asleep. Get up, read something calm in dim light, and go back to bed when you feel sleepy, without the phone.
  • The phone is your alarm. Put it across the room. You’ll still hear it, and you’ll have to get up to turn it off.
  • You slipped. Put the phone down as soon as you notice, log the slip, and don’t make up for it by staying up later. Tomorrow night the count starts again.
