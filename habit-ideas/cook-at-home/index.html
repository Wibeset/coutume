How to cook dinner at home

Last updated: November 21, 2026

The hard part of cooking at home usually isn’t the cooking. It’s 6 p.m., you’re hungry, the fridge looks empty and you have no idea what to make, so takeout wins.

Why it’s worth it

Cooking at home lets you choose what goes on your plate, usually costs less than ordering in, and gives the end of the day a routine. Once you have a handful of meals you can make without thinking, it takes less effort than deciding where to order from.

How to start

  1. Keep a list of five easy meals. Dishes you can make in 30 minutes without a recipe: pasta, an omelette, a stir-fry, tacos, soup. On a tired night, pick from the list instead of inventing.
  2. Decide before you shop. Choose a few dinners for the week and buy for those. Knowing what’s for dinner is half the work.
  3. Stock a backup shelf. Pasta, canned tomatoes, beans, rice, eggs, frozen vegetables. With these, there’s always something to make.
  4. Cook once, eat twice. Make a double batch and keep the rest for tomorrow’s lunch or a night when you can’t face the stove.
  5. Lower the bar. Eggs on toast with a salad is a home-cooked dinner. Simple counts.

When it gets hard

  • You’re too tired. Reheat leftovers or make the simplest meal on your list. Tonight, putting a plate together counts as cooking.
  • You don’t know what to make. Look at what needs using up in the fridge first, then pick the easy meal that fits.
  • You’re eating out with friends. Enjoy it. One evening out doesn’t undo the habit; cook again the next night.
