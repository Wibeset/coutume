How long does it take to form a habit?

Last updated: November 23, 2026

About 66 days on average, not 21. And it varies a lot: from 18 days to more than 250, depending on the person and the habit. The good news: missing a day doesn’t send you back to zero.

Where the 21 days come from

The “21 days” figure goes back to the 1960s, to a plastic surgeon, Maxwell Maltz, who noticed that his patients took a minimum of about 21 days to get used to a change. It was an observation, not a measurement of habits, and “a minimum of 21 days” became “21 days” over the years.

What the research found

In a study published online in 2009 (in print in 2010), a team at University College London led by Phillippa Lally followed 96 people for 84 days. Each chose a new daily behaviour, like drinking a glass of water with lunch or going for a run before dinner, and reported every day how automatic it felt.

  • On average, it took 66 days for the behaviour to become as automatic as it would get.
  • The range was wide: from 18 days to an estimated 254 days.
  • Simple habits, like drinking water, became automatic sooner than demanding ones, like exercising.
  • Missing one opportunity didn’t meaningfully affect the process. What prevented a habit from forming was being inconsistent, not missing a day.

Source: Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C. H. M., Potts, H. W. W. and Wardle, J. (2010). How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world. European Journal of Social Psychology, 40(6), 998–1009.

What it means for you

  • Plan for two months, not three weeks. If a habit still takes effort after 21 days, nothing is wrong with you.
  • Make it easy. The simpler the behaviour, the sooner it becomes automatic: start with a glass of water or 5 minutes, then grow.
  • Same time, same cue. Repeating a behaviour in the same situation, after coffee or before bed, is what makes it automatic.
  • Forgive the missed day, then come back. One miss barely matters; a long break does.
